For this sound visualization, I wanted to use volume to control the movement of particles and create changes in the image. I wanted the particles to move up and down like waves, following the intensity of the music. Initially, I planned to place a grid of white particles in the center of the screen and control their vertical movement. But I wanted the movement to feel less rigid, so I created three offset layers of particles. And I discovered that adding a layer of black particles covering a larger area, and making them move up and down as well, could create a flickering effect，because the black and white particles moved past each other. When the volume increases, the particles move over a larger range; when the volume decreases, the particles gradually becomes flatter. The interface includes buttons to return to the beginning, play/pause, stop, and a volume slider.
When testing with music, I found that during sections with very low volume or silence, the image could gradually turn black. This was a small surprise, and I felt it added a change in the visual rhythm. Playing music with a strong beat for a long time actually made the visual changes feel repetitive and left me feeling a little dizzy. However, with fewer volume change music, the visuals were less moving because the particles’ vertical movement was smaller, so I think I should explore other ways of controlling this situation to get more variation.
